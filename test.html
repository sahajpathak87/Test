A Garden Notebook

A working reference for core HTML tags, each shown against a page from a birdwatcher's autumn notebook — live on the left, markup on the right.

<h1>–<h6>

Headings

Six levels of heading, used here as a journal entry's own hierarchy — title, date, species, then finer notes.

Garden Log — Week 12

October 3rd, overcast, 9°C

Eurasian Blackbird

Juvenile, seen near the hedgerow

Feeding on windfall apples
Confirmed against the field guide, p. 114
<h1>Garden Log — Week 12</h1>
<h2>October 3rd, overcast, 9°C</h2>
<h3>Eurasian Blackbird</h3>
<h4>Juvenile, seen near the hedgerow</h4>
<h5>Feeding on windfall apples</h5>
<h6>Confirmed against the field guide, p. 114</h6>
<p>

Paragraph

Groups a block of related text — the main unit of writing in a page.

Arrived at the garden just after eight. The blackbirds were already busy in the apple windfall, and a pair of long-tailed tits passed through the birch without stopping.

<p>Arrived at the garden just after eight. The blackbirds
were already busy in the apple windfall, and a pair of
long-tailed tits passed through the birch without stopping.</p>
<br />

Line Break

Forces a line to end without starting a new paragraph — useful for short, address-like lines.

Site: Willow Garden, plot 4
Weather: overcast, light wind
Observer: R. Kalyan 

<p>
  Site: Willow Garden, plot 4<br />
  Weather: overcast, light wind<br />
  Observer: R. Kalyan
</p>
<hr />

Horizontal Rule

Draws a thematic break between two sections — here, separating morning notes from afternoon notes.

Morning: quiet, only the resident robin singing.

Afternoon: a small flock of starlings passed over, heading south.

<p>Morning: quiet, only the resident robin singing.</p>
<hr />
<p>Afternoon: a small flock of starlings passed over, heading south.</p>

Comment

Notes left in the markup for whoever reads the source later. The browser skips them entirely — nothing below is visible on the page, only in the code.

Nothing to see here — the comment beside this text is invisible on the page.

<ol>

Ordered List

A numbered sequence — right when the order itself carries meaning, like steps in a process.

  1. Stand still and let the garden settle for a minute.
  2. Note the light, temperature, and wind.
  3. Record each species as it's confirmed, not guessed.
  4. Sketch anything unfamiliar before it moves on.
<ol>
  <li>Stand still and let the garden settle for a minute.</li>
  <li>Note the light, temperature, and wind.</li>
  <li>Record each species as it's confirmed, not guessed.</li>
  <li>Sketch anything unfamiliar before it moves on.</li>
</ol>
<ul>

Unordered List

A list where the order doesn't matter — a set of items rather than a sequence.

  • Field notebook and pencil
  • Binoculars, 8×42
  • Pocket field guide
  • Flask of tea

  <li>Binoculars, 8×42</li>
  <li>Pocket field guide</li>
  <li>Flask of tea</li>
</ul>
